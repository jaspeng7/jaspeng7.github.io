About this policy

MyQuarter is a personal goal and planning app for iPhone and Mac. It is made by Jason Peng, an independent developer based in Australia ("we", "us"). This policy covers the iPhone app, its widgets and the Mac app. It explains what the app collects, why, where it goes, how long it is kept, and how you can see, change or delete it.

You use one account on every device. What you write is stored on our servers, not only on the device you wrote it on, so the same vision, goals, plans and writing appear on each iPhone or Mac you sign in on. Where the iPhone and Mac apps collect or keep different things, this policy says so.

MyQuarter is in a free, invite-only beta. As the app changes, this policy will change with it.

The short version

  • What you write in MyQuarter is yours. We use it to run the app for you, and for nothing else.
  • We do not sell your information, show you ads, or track you across other companies' apps and websites.
  • To see where the app is confusing or broken, we record how you use it: which screens you open, and reports of errors. On iPhone, we also record where you tap, recordings of the screen with every word hidden, and reports of crashes and freezes. Never what you type, unless you choose to send us feedback.
  • Some features send parts of what you have written to an AI provider (currently Google's Gemini) to write a suggestion or a summary. None of them runs until you allow it, and you can turn them all off in Settings. Once allowed, three of them run on their own. All of them are listed below.
  • Your account and what you write are stored in Sydney, Australia. Some of the services we use are in other countries, mostly the United States.
  • You can delete your account, and everything you have written, at any time in Settings.

What we collect

Information you give us:

  • Account details: your email address and first name, and a password if you sign in with email. Passwords are stored by our sign-in provider only in scrambled (hashed) form. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your name and email address from them. Apple may give us a private relay address instead of your real one, if you choose that.
  • What you write in the app: your vision across five life areas (physical, mental, relational, financial and professional), including your answers to prompts such as what you will no longer tolerate; your goals, due dates and actions, including short notes you add to an action's days (for example "Long run" on a Wednesday), which are shown only to you, never used to work out your score and never sent to an AI model; the days you tick an action off, and how many times; your habits, the life area you file each one under, and your habit check-ins; your daily and weekly reflections; the notes you write in your journal; your priority cards, including any name you enter when you hand a card to someone; and how each goal turned out at the end of a season.
  • Your settings: theme, reminder and notification choices and times, time zone, and whether AI coaching is on.
  • Information the app makes from yours: execution scores calculated from your ticks, and AI-written area summaries and weekly strengths and concerns, which are saved with your writing so they do not have to be written twice.
  • Feedback you send: if you use Send feedback in the app, the note you write and the type you pick. See "Analytics".

Information collected automatically:

  • Usage analytics: when you open and close the app, which screens you open, and when you complete key steps, such as finishing onboarding, creating a goal, ticking off an action or a habit, or asking for an AI suggestion. On iPhone, also which buttons you tap, which widgets you have added, and when you use them. See "Analytics".
  • Screen recordings, on iPhone only: how the app's screens look while you use them and where you tap, with every word on the screen hidden. See "Analytics".
  • Crash and error reports: technical details of a crash or a freeze (on iPhone) or of a request that failed (on iPhone and Mac). See "Analytics".
  • Technical information: your IP address, device model, operating system and its version (iOS or macOS), app version, and the times you use the app. Our database and analytics providers receive this as part of running their services.

Before you create an account: you can write your vision and set your first goal before you sign up. Your vision is kept on your device. Your goal and its actions are saved on our servers under an anonymous sign-in, which also lets the AI features work. When you create your account, that anonymous sign-in becomes your account, with your goal in it. If you sign in to an account you already had, the goals you set up before signing in move into it when it has no season yet. Otherwise they stay with the anonymous sign-in on our servers. An anonymous sign-in that never becomes an account, and anything left under it, stays on our servers until we remove it. There is no automatic clean-up for these yet.

Sensitive information

The app asks you to think about your health, your money and your relationships. What you write may include health information or other information that privacy laws treat as sensitive.

We only have it because you choose to write it, and we only use it to provide the features you use. By writing it in the app, you agree to us storing it. It is sent to our AI provider, for the AI features described below, only if you allow them. Nothing sensitive is needed for the app to work, and you can edit or delete what you have written at any time.

We never use it for advertising, never sell it, and never share it with anyone other than the service providers listed below, who handle it on our behalf.

If you write about other people, such as a partner, a family member or a colleague, please keep it to what you need for your own planning.

How we use your information

  • To run the app: store your writing, calculate your scores, show your plans and reflections back to you, and keep your account secure.
  • To produce the AI features described below.
  • To understand how the app is used and improve it, using analytics.
  • To contact you about your account, the beta, or changes to this policy or our terms.
  • To meet our legal obligations and enforce our Terms of service.

The app makes no automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you. The AI features only write suggestions and summaries for you to read.

AI features

Some features use an AI model to write a suggestion or a summary. The request goes from our server to the AI provider, and only the text that feature needs is sent. We do not add your name or email address to these requests, but your own writing is sent as you wrote it, including any names or details in it.

Our AI provider is currently Google (the Gemini API). Google may process these requests outside Australia, including in the United States, under its Gemini API terms. We use Gemini's paid service, so Google does not use what we send it, or what it sends back, to improve its products; it keeps them for a limited time to detect abuse. Anthropic (the Claude API) is set up as a backup and is only used if we switch to it. We do not train any AI model on your information.

What each feature sends:

  • Area summaries (automatic): when you move away from one area of your vision, that area's text is sent to write the one-line summary shown on its card. This happens without you tapping anything.
  • Weekly strengths and concerns (automatic): when you open a week's review, we send the week number, your execution score, the actions you committed to with the goal each belongs to and how many you completed, and the daily reflections you wrote that week. The result is saved on that week so it is only written once.
  • End-of-season goal summaries (automatic): when you reach the "Season complete" screen at the end of your twelve weeks, each goal's title, its score, and its actions with your week-by-week counts are sent to write a short summary.
  • Get feedback on this (when you tap it): your answers to the vision prompts for every area you have written, plus the text you are writing at the time, are sent to write a short coaching note.
  • Suggest goals from your vision (when you tap it): the text of the vision area you choose, and the titles of the goals you already have, are sent to write goal ideas.
  • Suggest actions (when you tap it, while choosing a goal's weekly actions): the goal's title and due date, the names of the actions it already has, and the text of the vision area it belongs to (or just the goal's title, if it has none) are sent to write three suggested actions. Nothing is added to your goal unless you tap a suggestion.
  • Check my week (when you tap it, while planning your first week): your goals' titles, each action's name, weekly count and days, your answers to the four reality-check questions (your free time on a weekday and on a weekend day, when you have the most energy, and which days are already full), and the text of your vision are sent to estimate how many hours your week asks for and suggest a time for each action. Your answers are not saved, and nothing is added to your plan or your calendar unless you do it yourself.

AI features run only after you allow them, and you can turn them all off in Settings. The first time one would run, the app asks "Use AI coaching?" and sends nothing unless you tap "Allow AI coaching". If you tap "Not now", or later switch off "Use AI coaching" in Settings, none of your writing is sent to an AI provider, the automatic features included. Each iPhone or Mac you sign in on asks before it sends anything, and switching AI coaching off on one turns it off on all of them.

AI providers may keep requests and responses for a limited time, for example to detect abuse, under their own terms.

To keep the AI features working and their cost in check, our server records a few facts about each AI request in PostHog (see "Analytics"): which feature it was, which AI model answered, how long it took, how many words it used (counted in tokens), what it cost, and whether it worked. It never records what you wrote or what the AI wrote back.

Analytics

We use PostHog, on its United States cloud, to understand how the app is used and where it goes wrong, for example how many people finish onboarding or tick off an action. The app sends PostHog the name of each screen you open and named events such as "goal created" or "lead action ticked", sometimes with a number or a label: the week number, a score, or which life area or AI feature was used.

When you are signed in, these are linked to a random account ID. PostHog also receives your name and email address, linked to your account, so we can tell which person feedback and usage come from. We never use them for marketing, and never sell them. Before you create an account, PostHog does not have your name or email address. We never send PostHog anything you write, except a feedback note you choose to send (below).

Screen recordings (session replay), on iPhone only: PostHog records how the app's screens look while you use them, and where you tap, so we can see where the app is confusing. Every word on the screen is hidden on your phone before the recording is sent: what you type, and the words already on the screen, such as your goals, your vision and your journal, show up as blank bars. The layout, the colours, the pictures and your taps are recorded. The keyboard is not. The Mac app does not record the screen.

Taps, on iPhone only: PostHog notes which buttons you tap, by the name we gave each button in the app's code, never by what the button shows. It also notes when the same spot is tapped several times in a row, which tells us something looked tappable and did not respond.

Crash and error reports: if the app crashes on your iPhone, it sends PostHog a crash report the next time you open it. If the app freezes on your iPhone for more than a couple of seconds, or iOS closes it because it stopped responding, Apple's diagnostics tell the app where it was stuck, and the app sends that to PostHog the next time you open it. The Mac app does not send crash or freeze reports of its own (see "Emails and notifications" for the ones Apple may share with us). On iPhone and Mac, if a request to our server fails in a way you can see, the app sends a short error report. These reports contain technical details only: what failed, where in the app's code, and the error code. Never what you wrote.

Widgets, on iPhone only (the Mac app has none): which MyQuarter widgets you have added to your home and lock screens, and when you tap one. A tick you make on a widget is noted on your phone and sent the next time you open the app.

Send feedback: if you send a note from the app, PostHog receives its text, the type you picked (if any), the app version and the name of the screen you were on, linked to your account. We read these notes to improve MyQuarter. To have one deleted, email us. If you are offline, the note waits on your device and is sent when you are back online.

PostHog also receives your IP address, as any internet service does, and basic details about your device, such as its model, operating system and its version (iOS or macOS), language and app version. It may use the IP address to estimate a rough location. The app sends all of this through a small relay on our own server (Supabase), which passes it straight to PostHog and does not store it, apart from the short-term server logs described under "How long we keep it".

PostHog keeps a random identifier on each device so it can tell visits apart. It is replaced when you sign out or delete your account. We do not use analytics for advertising.

Emails and notifications

Account emails, such as password resets, are sent by our sign-in provider, Supabase.

Reminder emails: the app does not send reminder emails. If that changes, we will update this policy first.

Notifications: if you allow them, the app's reminders (the weekly review, closing your day, a habit at risk) are scheduled by the app on your iPhone or Mac, from the notification choices in Settings, and shown by iOS or macOS. Each device asks for permission separately and shows no reminders until you allow it. Nothing is sent to a server to deliver them, and no push service is used. A habit reminder shows the habit's name, so it can appear on your lock screen or in Notification Centre. Signing out or deleting your account removes them from that device.

If you test MyQuarter through TestFlight, on iPhone or Mac, Apple shares with us the crash reports and the feedback you choose to send through TestFlight, under Apple's own terms. If you install MyQuarter from the App Store and have chosen in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also share crash reports with us.

Storage on your device

The app keeps some information on each iPhone or Mac you use it on, so it works smoothly: your sign-in, kept in the Keychain; your theme; which introductory screens you have seen; what you are writing before it saves (your name and vision before you sign up, today's reflection, a vision edit, a feedback note you have not sent); and the last coaching notes and goal ideas the app showed you, together with the vision text they were written for. PostHog also keeps a random identifier, as described above, and holds analytics (and, on iPhone, screen recordings and crash reports) on the device until they are sent. On iPhone, the app shares a space with its widgets, where they keep your sign-in, a copy of the day the widgets show (your actions, habits and vision), and widget taps waiting to be counted.

Signing out removes your sign-in, any unsaved reflection, vision edit or feedback note, and on iPhone the widgets' copy of your day, from that device. Deleting your account removes everything the app keeps on the device you delete it from.

Removing the app: when you delete MyQuarter from an iPhone, iOS removes what the app keeps there, apart from your sign-in, which iOS keeps in the Keychain. If you install MyQuarter again, the app deletes that old sign-in when it first opens. On a Mac, moving MyQuarter to the Bin does not remove what it keeps on the Mac, your sign-in included. Sign out first to remove your sign-in and unsaved writing, or delete your account to remove everything the app keeps on that Mac.

Add to calendar: the first time, you choose Google Calendar or Apple Calendar, and the app remembers that on the device. With Google Calendar, the app opens Google Calendar's own new-event page, in its app or your web browser, filled in with the action, that day's note if you wrote one, its goal title, the time and how many times you have done it this week, so Google receives those details. With Apple Calendar, the app either opens Apple's own event screen, filled in the same way, or asks once for permission to add events and then adds them to your default calendar itself. That permission lets the app add events, never see the ones already there. The calendar's own service (for example iCloud or Google) receives the event if it syncs. We save the date and time you picked so the app can show it back to you. The app does not read your calendar.

Who we share it with

We do not sell or rent your personal information. We share it only with the service providers that run parts of the app, and only what each one needs:

  • Supabase: database, sign-in, account emails and server functions. Your account and everything you write are stored in Supabase's Sydney, Australia region. Server functions, including the ones that pass requests to our AI provider, may run outside Australia.
  • Google (Gemini API): the AI features.
  • Anthropic (Claude API): backup AI provider, only used if we switch to it.
  • PostHog: product analytics, linked to your name and email address, screen recordings, and crash and error reports, on its United States cloud.
  • Google or Apple: sign-in, only if you choose to sign in with them.
  • Apple: TestFlight crash reports and feedback, if you test the beta on iPhone or Mac, and App Store crash reports if you choose to share them.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or if it is needed to protect someone's safety. If MyQuarter is ever transferred to someone else, we will tell you before your information moves, and this policy will continue to apply to it.

Storing information overseas

Your account and your writing are stored in Australia. Some of the providers above handle information in other countries, mostly the United States. We use established providers whose terms include data protection commitments.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you write: until you delete it, or delete your account.
  • Anonymous sign-ins that never become an account: until we remove them. See "What we collect".
  • What the app keeps on your iPhone or Mac: until you sign out, delete your account or remove the app. See "Storage on your device".
  • Copies held by our providers, such as server logs, short-term backups, analytics and AI provider records: for the limited time their own terms and settings allow. When an AI feature fails, server logs can occasionally contain part of the AI's response.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in Settings, on iPhone or Mac. You type DELETE to confirm, and the deletion happens straight away. There is no grace period and it cannot be undone.

It permanently deletes your profile and everything you have written or recorded in the app: your vision, goals, actions, ticks, habits, weekly plans, daily and weekly reflections, priority cards, calendar entries, settings and notification choices.

It also deletes your sign-in record (your email address, name, password and any linked Google or Apple sign-in), removes your email address from the beta invite list, and clears what the app keeps on the device you delete it from. Your account is gone for every device at once, but each other iPhone or Mac you used keeps what the app stored on it until you sign out there, so sign out on your other devices first. Copies held by our providers, as described above, are not removed by the button. To have your analytics deleted as well, including the name and email address PostHog holds, email myquarterapp@gmail.com.

You can also restart a season in Settings, which removes its ticks and weekly plans and, if you choose, its goals and actions and your vision. What you wrote in your daily and weekly reflections is kept, as notes in your journal, and your habits and account are not touched. It cannot be undone.

Your rights

You can see and edit most of your information in the app, and delete all of it in Settings. There is no self-service export yet. To get a copy of your data, to correct something, to ask what we hold about you, or to object to how we use it, email myquarterapp@gmail.com. We will reply within 30 days and will not charge you. We may need to confirm the request comes from you first.

If you are not happy with our reply, you can complain to the privacy or data protection regulator where you live.

Keeping it secure

Database rules mean each account can only read and write its own records. Connections between the app and our servers are encrypted (HTTPS). The keys for our AI provider are kept on the server and are never part of the app on your devices. The AI features only answer a signed-in session (including the anonymous one used before you create an account), and most are limited to a set number of uses per season.

As the operator, we can technically access the database. We only do so to maintain the app, fix a problem, respond to a request from you, or where the law requires it.

No system is perfectly secure. If a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm, we will tell you, and the regulator where the law requires it.

Children

You must be 18 or older to use MyQuarter. It is not directed at children or teenagers, and we do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has created an account, email myquarterapp@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

We will update this policy as the app changes. If a change is significant, we will tell you in the app or by email before it takes effect. The date at the top shows when it last changed.
